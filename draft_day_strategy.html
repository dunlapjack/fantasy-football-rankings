Stop drafting best available

Your board sorts by VOR, computed once against an empty roster. It never learns what you already have — so in round 8 it still prices RB5 as if you had none. Across 120 simulated drafts per league, pure best-available finished last of the five model-based strategies in all four of your leagues.

The rule: take the best player who would change your starting lineup — unless someone else is so much better that waiting on his position costs you more.

Everything below is that sentence, priced out. Bars show season points gained or lost against pure best-available off the v17 board.

Three things that surprised the simulator

Caps barely help

Bolting "max 5 RB" onto best-available gained just +9 / +4 / +1 / +2 points. Capping is a rule about counting; the problem is about value.

One tight end is the real mistake

Forcing a hard cap at each position, the board is almost flat — except stopping at one TE costs ~12 points and stopping at three RB costs ~8. You don't need a precise roster shape. You need to stop doing the one bad thing.

Your board is not the market

In the 12-team league ADP drafts RB3.4 / WR7.7. Your board's best-available drafts RB7.9 / WR2.5 — near mirror images. Phase 13 said trust the model's ordering of backs, not how many. This is the cost of not listening.

Method. src/draft_sim.py — 120 drafts per policy per league from your real slot, opponents on ADP + each player's own adp_stdev, then the regular season played week by week: byes certain, injuries a Markov chain, lineup filled optimally, unfillable slots streamed from the third-best undrafted player. Common random numbers across policies, so differences are the policy and not the luck. Re-run at three injury levels — the ordering held in all twelve runs.

Caveat. Everything is measured in the board's own projections; if a projection is wrong this drafts the wrong player in a well-constructed way. Optimises points, not wins.
